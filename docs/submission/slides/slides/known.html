The boxes were fixed before the first was looked at. A drought that comes and goes inside the period is not a 25-year trend, and VIGIL does not report one. A box we chose gives exploratory p-values, like any zone.

This is the zone feature pointed at ourselves. Three come out as documented: the Loess Plateau, the southern Amazon and the Sahel. The Himalaya box is warming, but at +0.20 K per decade with a wide interval it does not show warming faster than the globe. California and the Murray-Darling show the limit of a trend test: both droughts are real, but neither is a steady 25-year change in rainfall, so a tool that reported one would be wrong. Change points are the right tool there. Every trend named on this slide has a p-value under 0.05 for its box, uncorrected. The file with every variable for every box is in the repository under docs/submission/checks. One more check worth telling: 56 of the 130 change points in sea temperature fall in mid-2013, which usually means a sensor fault. We measured the step in the ship-and-buoy record too: +0.78 K against the satellite’s +0.99 K, and close to zero in both four years either side. So it is the sea. The cells make three patches, and the one in the Gulf of Alaska is where the marine heatwave called the Blob was reported in late 2013.
