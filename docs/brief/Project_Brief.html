NASA INTERNATIONAL SPACE APPS CHALLENGE 2026 / EARTH SYSTEM TREND DETECTIVE

VIGIL

(Visualising & Identifying Global Indicators of Long-term change)

Project brief | An autonomous Earth-system trend discovery and investigation platform

Mission. VIGIL will search real Earth-observation records to discover significant environmental changes without being told which events to find. It will examine where, when and how Earth systems are changing, identify relationships and contradictions between variables, and produce transparent, reproducible evidence that users can interrogate.

Design principle: discovery first, explanation second. California drought, Himalayan warming and other well-known cases are potential outputs of the engine, not hard-coded examples or regions chosen to guarantee a compelling result.

01 | Five mandatory capabilities

The tag after each name is the stage by which that capability must work, and when the stage falls due. Section 02 sets out the stages.

1. Earth System Cascade Detection [INTELLIGENCE, SUNDAY 12:00]

Discover co-occurring or lagged changes across precipitation, temperature, soil moisture and vegetation. Build an evidence graph with measured direction, magnitude, timing and uncertainty. Describe these as associations unless independent causal evidence exists.

2. Statistical Significance Explorer [FOUNDATION, SATURDAY 17:30]

Calculate change rates, confidence intervals and significance for each eligible region/variable/time period. Visualise trend strength and uncertainty, distinguish statistical significance from practical magnitude, and expose raw values and methods.

3. AI Earth Detective [INTELLIGENCE, SUNDAY 12:00]

An in-software assistant that interprets a natural-language question, invokes authorised analysis tools, and answers only from computed results with dataset, geography, period, uncertainty and method references. It must say when evidence is inconclusive.

4. Environmental Change-Point Detection [INTELLIGENCE, SUNDAY 12:00]

Identify when a time series changes level or slope, distinguish abrupt shifts from long-term trends, estimate the change-point date and uncertainty, and check whether breakpoints coincide across variables.

5. Contradiction Finder [INTELLIGENCE, SUNDAY 12:00]

Search for nearby or comparable locations with opposite trends, e.g., one area greening while its neighbour browns. Rank by statistical reliability and comparability rather than visual drama, and flag plausible confounders.

Development structure: all five capabilities are required, and each of them needs harmonised data before it can produce anything real. The work is split into work areas by layer (data, analysis, interface, assistant), set out in section 08. Each capability has a thin version that must ship, listed in section 02.

03 | Discovery before demonstration

  • Define a neutral search space first: select a predeclared geographic grid or unbiased set of regions, supported variables and common observation windows based on coverage and data quality, not on famous events.
  • Screen systematically: process every eligible region using the same preprocessing, trend tests and thresholds; record non-significant, missing and contradictory results as well as discoveries.
  • Rank findings by a transparent score: combine effect size, uncertainty, coverage, robustness and cross-system relevance. The score is for prioritising investigation, not proof of a discovery.
  • Investigate what emerges: if a California water-stress pattern or Himalayan temperature anomaly ranks highly, drill down and explain it. If those cases do not emerge, present the actual highest-quality findings instead.
  • Separate discovery from validation: re-test shortlisted patterns on held-out years, alternative measures or an independent dataset where feasible; label exploratory results clearly when independent validation is unavailable.

Starting proposal for the scan

These settings are fixed before the scan runs and are not tuned afterwards to make a result appear. They are a proposal until the first downloads confirm coverage.

SettingProposal
Grid2.5° by 2.5° cells over land, which is roughly 3,000 cells.
PeriodJanuary 2001 to December 2025 in monthly steps, about 300 points per series.
EligibilityA cell and variable are scanned when at least 90% of months are present. Cells that are mostly ocean or permanent ice are left out.
SeriesThe area-weighted mean of the cell, then the anomaly from that cell's own monthly climatology.
ThresholdAdjusted p below 0.05 after false-discovery-rate control across every test in the scan.
RecordEvery cell and variable, whether significant or not, with the reason when a cell is skipped.

04 | Scientific defensibility: non-negotiable

  • Harmonise spatial resolution, monthly/seasonal aggregation, units, missing-data rules and overlapping date ranges before comparing variables. Avoid comparing unrelated pixels or time periods.
  • Account for seasonality, temporal autocorrelation and multiple testing across many regions (e.g., false-discovery-rate control); disclose adjusted significance and confidence intervals.
  • Use a documented method suited to the series: seasonal trend estimation, regression or Mann-Kendall with appropriate serial-dependence treatment; use validated change-point analysis and sensitivity checks.
  • For cascade claims, test lagged correlations and temporal ordering; control for shared seasonal cycles. Never imply that correlations establish causation.
  • Every reported finding must retain provenance: mission/product, variable, source URL/identifier, spatial extent, date range, preprocessing, test, parameters, results and known limitations.

How VIGIL plans to meet each rule

RulePlanned approach
HarmonisingThe pipeline averages every product onto the same grid and the same monthly steps over the same period. Units are converted once, and the conversion is recorded.
SeasonalityEach cell's monthly climatology is subtracted before any test runs, so tests see anomalies.
AutocorrelationA modified Mann-Kendall test with the Hamed-Rao variance correction, or pre-whitening. Both are in pymannkendall.
Multiple testingBenjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate control across all tests in the scan, from statsmodels. Both raw and adjusted p-values are kept.
Change pointsruptures on the anomaly series, rerun with a second penalty setting as the sensitivity check.
CascadesLagged correlation between anomaly series at lags of 0 to 6 months, reported with its lag and sign as an association.
ProvenanceEvery finding carries the fields listed in section 07.

Which autocorrelation correction to use is the main question to put to a mentor during Saturday's office hours.

Data flows right to left. The pipeline fills the catalogue, the engine scans it and writes the results cache, and the API serves that cache to the app. The AI Detective calls back into the API, so it reads the same results the app does.

  • The app never touches NASA data or statistics directly. It talks to the Python service over HTTP, exchanging JSON, using HttpClient and System.Text.Json. No NuGet packages are needed.
  • The global scan runs ahead of time and is stored, so the API mostly reads finished results. Only a rerun that the user requests for one region computes anything live.
  • The data pipeline uses xarray and rioxarray. PyQGIS was considered and dropped: it cannot be installed with pip and ties every laptop to a QGIS install, which is setup time the weekend does not have. QGIS remains useful as a desktop tool for checking a raster by eye.
  • The analysis engine uses pymannkendall, statsmodels, scipy and ruptures.
  • The AI Detective lives on the Python side and has no source of numbers other than the API. The app holds only its chat panel. The AI may select and explain analyses; it must never invent or directly overwrite the numerical outputs. Every result is reproducible without AI.
  • The result contract is the JSON format of a finding, and it is the only place where the C# and Python halves meet. A fixture file of fake results in that format is committed before the event so the app, the API and the assistant can all be built before real numbers exist.

The discovery run

The path from NASA's archive to finished findings is one job that runs in the cloud, and anyone can rerun it and get the same numbers.

  1. One cloud machine and one storage bucket, in Amazon's us-west-2 region. NASA's files are stored there, so reading them is fast and free. Downloading them over venue wifi is neither.
  2. The machine fetches the four products, one file per month from 2001 to 2025: about 1,200 files and 60 to 80 GB.
  3. It regrids each product onto the shared grid and saves the result to the bucket. This is the catalogue: for every grid cell, one number per month for each measurement. Everyone reads this one copy.
  4. It runs the analysis on every grid cell and saves the findings.
  5. Each run writes a run record beside its results: the version of the code, the settings, how many cells were checked, how many were skipped and why, and how long it took.
  6. The API is hosted on a web address, so the app, the AI Detective and anyone reviewing the project read the same results. It hands out results and never changes them.
  7. One command, written in the README, reruns the whole thing from nothing.

A small run comes first. One year of one product is fetched, regridded, saved, analysed and read back through the API. The full run starts only when that works from end to end, and its timing replaces the estimates below.

Untested estimates: fetching and regridding the full set takes 30 minutes to 2 hours and happens once. The analysis then takes 10 to 20 minutes, so the scan can be rerun several times in an afternoon.

Two passes

The scan decides where to look closely. We do not.

  • The coarse pass covers the whole planet in 250 km squares, every square treated the same. This is the scan described in section 03.
  • The fine pass takes the top-ranked squares, about 20 of them, and repeats the analysis inside each at the products' own detail: 10 km for rainfall and 5 km for vegetation. It shows which valleys and slopes drive a change, and it is where neighbouring contradictions become visible. It needs about 30 to 60 minutes, provided the original files are kept on the cloud machine.
  • The fine pass describes a finding and does not prove it a second time. A square is chosen because it already looked significant, so its detailed view is labelled exploratory.
  • The fine pass is stretch work. It starts only when the coarse scan is solid.

Known-answer check: after the scan has run, we look at whether it flagged places with well-documented changes, such as California and the Himalayas. Finding them is evidence the method works. Missing them is a reason to investigate, not proof of a fault: whether a known change shows up depends on the variables, the grid size, the period and the threshold. They are checked afterwards and never fed in beforehand.

Running without the service

  • When a scan finishes, its findings are saved as one snapshot file and shipped with the app.
  • If the service cannot be reached, the app loads the snapshot. Everything works except live reruns and the AI Detective.
  • The app is published as a single download that needs nothing installed, on the repository's releases page, with a short note on how to run it. A reviewer who downloads it sees real findings without installing Python or fetching data.
  • The same fallback protects the live demo if the service fails in front of the judges.

If the cloud run is late

  • Dan's home PC is the fallback. It is powerful enough for the regridding, and Dan can reach it remotely from his laptop at the venue.
  • Temperature and rainfall start downloading to it on Friday night, so the two products the Foundation stage needs are in hand before the event, whatever happens to the cloud setup.
  • Each download asks for the one field we use, not the whole file. A MERRA-2 monthly file holds dozens of measurements and we want one.
  • Only the regridding needs a strong machine. The catalogue it produces is a few hundred megabytes, and the analysis runs on it in minutes on a laptop.
  • The cloud run stays the plan, because it gives the hosted API and a run anyone can repeat. The PC keeps Saturday on schedule if the cloud setup overruns.

How the split works

  • The pipeline stays with Ethan, since the aim is to run it in the cloud. If it falls behind, Will and Dan can write scripts and send them to him, and the home PC in section 07 covers the first two products.
  • The analysis does not wait for real data. Will starts on made-up series where the answer is known: a flat line with noise, then the same line with a trend added. A test that finds a trend in pure noise is wrong, and this shows it at once.

  • Dan builds the interface against the fixture, and Will builds the AI Detective against it too, so neither waits for the scan. Dan keeps the chat panel, which is the C# side of the Detective.

Working rules

  • One owner per folder. Everyone is working with AI coding assistants, which rewrite files freely, and two of them in one file means a merge conflict. COORDINATION.md in the repository maps each work area to its paths and its owner.
  • An assistant that needs a change outside its owner's paths stops and asks. It does not make the edit.
  • The result contract changes only through Dan. A field renamed on one side stops the two halves talking. Dan also decides what gets cut.
  • Commits are small and pushed often, so a broken build can be traced to one change.
  • Every assistant reads AGENTS.md and COORDINATION.md at the start of a session.
  • Anyone who stops while others carry on tells the others before they go: what works, what is broken and what to pick up next.

10 | Demo and submission

Demo walk-through

The same sequence serves the recorded video and the live presentation at 15:30 on Sunday.

  1. Open VIGIL. The globe shows the hotspots from the stored scan. Read the numbers from the run record: how many cells were scanned, how many passed the threshold after correction and how many showed nothing.
  2. Open the top-ranked finding. It is whatever the ranking put first that morning, not a case picked in advance.
  3. Show its evidence: the series, the trend with its confidence interval, any change point, and the raw values and method.
  4. Show a linked variable in the same cell, with its lag. Call it an association, not a cause.
  5. Show one contradiction: two neighbouring cells trending in opposite directions.
  6. Ask the AI Detective a question it can answer, and point out the findings it cites. Then ask one the data cannot settle, and show it saying so.
  7. Open the provenance and limitations for the finding, and reproduce one figure from the stored data.
  8. Close by stating what was built before the event and where AI was used.
